# Patch Mesa, then unlock G8x cameras

The GMEM depth-restore GPU fault on the LG G8x is **unreported upstream and not fixed in Mesa `main`**, so upgrading Mesa alone will almost certainly not cure it. The likely fix is small: a **10–30 line clamp in `fd6_gmem.cc`** that limits each restore blit to the attachment's own size, carried as one downstream patch and sent upstream as an issue and MR. `FD_MESA_DEBUG=nogmem`, scoped to gnome-shell, stays the zero-maintenance fallback. Cameras are the biggest win still open, and the hardest. **No SM8150 CAMSS bring-up exists anywhere**, so the G8x would be the first. The main blocker is the VFE 175 write-master register deltas. After that, sensor drivers can be built from an out-of-tree IMX363 driver and the mainline S5K3M5 driver, and libcamera softISP handles the rest. Of the other features, NFC, the Hall sensor, gyro exposure, the charger, GPS and SMS/data are cheap. Video decode, call audio, haptics and suspend are moderate. Fingerprint, DisplayPort alt mode and DualScreen are research projects that should be deferred. All effort estimates below are judgement calls from the research notes, not sourced figures.

## A one-file Mesa patch beats waiting for upstream

Searches of the Mesa tracker for A640, SM8150, GMEM restore, out-of-bounds GMEM and freedreno-mutter found **no matching issue or MR**. The only open A640 issue concerns the downstream KGSL driver ([#11204](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/mesa/mesa/-/work_items/11204)). The sm8150-mainline tracker has a single, unrelated, closed GPU issue ([work item 3](https://gitlab.com/sm8150-mainline/linux/-/work_items/3)). The Redmi K20 Pro and Flutter-on-A640 reports from the brief could not be found, so treat them as unverified. Filing our crashdec output would give the developers new information. The bug class is well known, though. A wrong tile alignment for 3-CCU parts was fixed in 2021 ([!11928](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/mesa/mesa/-/merge_requests/11928)). A wrong A722 CCU count that made a block run off the end of a buffer was fixed on **2026-11-01** ([!45827](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/mesa/mesa/-/merge_requests/45827)). `source=CCU` points at the colour/depth cache block ([Mesa freedreno docs](https://docs.mesa3d.org/drivers/freedreno.html)).

Is a newer Mesa free of it? Probably not. No commit to `fd6_gmem.cc` or `freedreno_devices.py` since September 2025 touches restore bounds or the A640 entries. The changes in that period are gen8 CCU and preamble refactors, LRZ fixes, YUV GMEM and an fbfetch/msrtt fix ([commit history](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/mesa/mesa/-/commits/main/src/gallium/drivers/freedreno/a6xx/fd6_gmem.cc), [devices history](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/mesa/mesa/-/commits/main/src/freedreno/common/freedreno_devices.py)). Only `main` was audited, not the 26.3 stable notes item by item, so a one-evening test build is still worth doing. In current code, `emit_restore_blits()` issues one `BLIT_EVENT_LOAD` per attachment, bounded only by a scissor sized to `align(pfb->width,16) × align(pfb->height,4)`. That is the **framebuffer** size, not the size of the depth resource ([fd6_gmem.cc](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/mesa/mesa/-/raw/main/src/gallium/drivers/freedreno/a6xx/fd6_gmem.cc)). The fault is a read at depth base + 0xb5000, about row 161 of a 112-row buffer. It fits mutter attaching a depth buffer smaller than the framebuffer. It also fits the hardware ignoring the scissor on the depth load path. `noubwc` making it worse favours the first explanation: a linear layout is smaller, so the overrun leaves the BO sooner.

The fix comes in three steps. First, capture the faulting submit with cffdump and compare `RB_RESOLVE_CNTL_2` and the bin window with the depth BO size. Second, in `emit_restore_blits()` (and the matching resolve path), clamp the scissor per attachment to `min(pfb, u_minify(height0, level))`, or skip the restore when the attachment is smaller than the framebuffer. Third, file the issue and MR so the patch can be dropped later. Maintenance is low: one file, with conflicts only when the blit-scissor helpers get refactored, as happened in December 2025. Also check that Linux 7.0 has the A640 GBIF CX restore fix ([freedreno list](http://www.mail-archive.com/freedreno@lists.freedesktop.org/msg48493.html)). Until the fix lands, `nogmem` forces the sysmem bypass that autotune already picks for many batches ([!9535](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/mesa/mesa/-/merge_requests/9535)). It costs some DRAM bandwidth on full-screen blended passes, and no measured power numbers exist yet. Hangs stay fatal in either case, because mutter still cannot recover from a GPU reset as of July 2026 ([GNOME blog](https://blogs.gnome.org/anonymoux47/2026/07/02/gpu-reset-recovery-in-mutter-a-progress-update/)). Zink with Turnip uses a separate GMEM load path but adds its own risks, so try it only after `nogmem`.

## Cameras hinge on porting VFE 175 write-masters

Lore and the community trees show **no SM8150 CAMSS patch**. The Pixel 4 wiki lists its camera as unsupported ([pmOS wiki](https://wiki.postmarketos.org/wiki/Google_Pixel_4_(google-flame))). The nearest upstream relatives are SDM845's vfe-170 ([camss-vfe-170.c](https://grok.openbmc.org/xref/openbmc/linux/drivers/media/platform/qcom/camss/camss-vfe-170.c?r=1083681e)) and newer SoCs such as SM6350 ([LKML](https://lkml.iu.edu/2511.1/05007.html)). Upstream uses only the CSIPHY → CSID → IFE RDI raw path and leaves Titan's ICP firmware CPU unused ([memcpy.io](https://memcpy.io/upstream-camera-support-for-qualcomm-platforms.html)). The current symptom is IRQs firing with no buf_done. That fits a write-master that is never enabled or committed, or a bus IRQ status register at a different offset. The reference for the deltas is downstream `cam_vfe175.h` against `cam_vfe170.h`. One downstream commit adds missing VFE 175 register data ([e/OS OnePlus sm8150](https://gitlab.e.foundation/e/devices/android_kernel_oneplus_sm8150/-/commit/7092864785b40117340438ae0b7bbe6f9c01deeb)). Another shows the UBWC `bw_limit` fields ([FP3 commit](https://gitlab.e.foundation/e/devices/android_kernel_fairphone_FP3/-/commit/95551cea14c80db00e0702993894651b9ad56a67)). Validate with the CSID test pattern generator before attaching any sensor, and dump the write-master registers after streamon.

Sensors fall into three tiers. The **IMX363** (main, 0x10 on the G8x) has a working out-of-tree driver in sdm670-mainline, used on the Pixel 3a ([tag](https://gitlab.com/sdm670-mainline/linux/-/tags/sdm670-v6.13.3)). It has no VCM focus support ([issue 2](https://gitlab.com/sdm670-mainline/linux/-/issues/2)), and libcamera will not merge its helper until the kernel driver is headed upstream ([patchwork 28201](https://patchwork.libcamera.org/patch/28201/)). The **S5K3M3-family wide sensor (0x30D3)** is a sibling of the S5K3M5, which mainline has supported since about 7.0 through `s5k3m5.c` ([linuxtv-commits](https://mail-archive.com/linuxtv-commits@linuxtv.org/msg48354.html)). Forking it with a new chip ID and mode tables taken from downstream is the cheapest new driver. The **S5KGD1 front sensor** (32 MP, tetracell) has no Linux driver at all. Its init tables must come from LG camx sensor-module blobs or an Android I2C trace, which makes it the highest-risk item. On userspace, libcamera supports CAMSS today through the simple pipeline with softISP. A dedicated CAMSS pipeline handler is under review ([patchwork 27432](https://patchwork.libcamera.org/patch/27432/)), and 0.7.1 adds multi-threaded debayering ([Phoronix](https://phoronix.com/news/libcamera-0.7.1-Released)). Preview should use binned modes, because softISP is CPU-bound. LineageOS has official mh2lm pages ([wiki](https://wiki.lineageos.org/devices/mh2lm/variant1/)), and its LGE sm8150 kernel and DTS remain the source for lane maps, regulators, resets, MCLK and CCI assignments. **No Project Renegade camera material was found.** The 0x3e AF/OIS part is unidentified. The EEPROM at 0x50 can be read through at24, but nothing upstream uses its data.

Fingerprint deserves a direct verdict. It would need a new qseecom client to load LG's signed trustlet, a protocol reverse-engineered from the Android HAL, a libfprint driver, and panel brightening under the finger ([emainline](https://emainline.gitlab.io/2021/12/12/fingerprint_P1.html)). A wrong reserved-memory access makes TZ halt the kernel ([sdm845 #30](https://gitlab.com/sdm845-mainline/linux/-/issues/30)). On a phone that needs a battery drain to recover from a hang, that is a poor trade.

## Prioritised plan

Start with **tier 1, about the first week**. Ship `nogmem` for gnome-shell now. Capture a cffdump, write the restore-clamp patch, test a Mesa `main` build, and file the issue. Add the Hall sensor, the NFC DT node, ssc-uinput for the gyro, smb5 charger enablement, and GPS through ModemManager. **Tier 2, weeks 2–4**, covers user-visible essentials: SMS and data with a SIM, bisecting the ADSP mic race (retry the q6afe start, or reload the NGD after PDR is up), then VFE 175 write-master bring-up with the CSID test pattern. **Tier 3, weeks 4–8**: the S5K3M3 driver forked from s5k3m5, the IMX363 port, libcamera softISP, a forward-port of the Nabu iris patch, the DW7800 haptics driver, and suspend measurement. **Tier 4** is opportunistic: call audio once the q6voice redesign lands, the S5KGD1 front camera, the fuel gauge, and wireless-charging status. Defer fingerprint, DP alt mode and DualScreen indefinitely. Every new probe touching TZ, SMMU or power sequencing must be designed not to hang, and needs the user's go-ahead first.

## Conclusion

The G8x's remaining gaps fall into two groups. Some are missing glue on top of drivers that already exist upstream, such as NFC, the charger, GPS, softISP, and S5K3M5 as a template. Others are missing first-of-kind SoC enablement where no SM8150 device has gone before: VFE 175, Iris1 and QSEE. The first group is days of work. The second is where this project could become the upstream reference for every SM8150 phone, and the camera stack is the most valuable place to do that. The Mesa bug is a cheap chance to contribute upstream and should not be treated as a blocker. Until someone captures and reports it, no amount of Mesa upgrades will remove it.
